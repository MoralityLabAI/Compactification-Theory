## Research question and honest information structure

Does leaking the *whole* 256-bit audit root BEFORE the attacker commits a changed-coordinate set allow the attacker to reduce detection, even though SHA-256 preimage commitments and the root sampler are assumed cryptographically secure? Distinguish a **batch-fixed attack**, using ONE four-coordinate set across a source's 512 audit trials, from a stronger **schedule-by-schedule attacker** that may choose a different set after seeing every realized audit.

The three treatment regimes are:
1. **Blind precommit**: attacker chooses 32 independently drawn source-uniform radius-four changed-coordinate sets per public source and binds all 32 with 256-bit random-nonce salted SHA-256 commitments BEFORE the OS audit root is drawn. These attacks cannot change afterward.
2. **Early root compromise**: after the CHALLENGE job fixes and discloses the OS root and 512 schedules per source, the attacker chooses 32 approximate best-response radius-four sets **per source and per auditor**, minimizing observed schedule exposure greedily over the FULL 512-schedule evaluation batch, ties independently shuffled. One altered set stays fixed across all 512 schedules. The attacker then makes new 256-bit salted commitments to the late attacks. Attempting to open any old precommitment with these late positions must fail. This is intentionally IN-SAMPLE schedule optimization, and its measured detection is not out-of-sample generalization.
3. **Per-challenge informed adversary**: choosing four uninspected coordinates separately after every realized 16-probe schedule always evades whenever enough coordinates are uninspected. This adversary can only issue a NEW commitment after disclosure, and cannot reopen an old blind commitment. It is a different timing regime.

The adversary's access to source/public audit distribution is unlimited in the second regime; the baseline blind sets are source-uniform independent of the root. The exact target coefficient witness remains external and authentic. SHA-256 binding, HMAC pseudorandomness and GitHub job integrity are assumptions rather than proved properties.

## Attack commitment and optimization

Each preimage also has a separate 256-bit random nonce from secrets.token_bytes(32). Store the public manifest and sealed original preimages separately; verify their SHA-256 hashes before minting the audit root. Any changed original index or nonce must fail to open its commitment.

After the OS-root schedules are written and SHA-256 frozen, the early-root attacker computes one integer trial-exposure bitmask per source/coordinate/auditor. It greedily chooses four distinct positions that minimize the number of the 512 audit schedules intersecting the selected set, tie orders shuffled with SHA256 stream **202611290102**, source digest, auditor and replica 0..31. Exactly **24×3×32=2304** late attack sets and new salted SHA-256 commitments are produced. These are not allowed to replace or open the original 768 precommitments. Keep every replica even if attack choices duplicate, and record distinct counts. An exact minimum four-set cover is not claimed; the rule is a registered greedy approximation. Each late attack is fixed for all 512 evaluations for that source/auditor. The selector receives full schedules: it is an intentionally compromised counterfactual with no held-out seed secrecy.

For each source/auditor, evaluate all 32 blind and all 32 late attack sets across the same 512 schedules. Thus **24×3×2×32×512 = 2,359,296** distinct fixed-batch attack detection decisions. ## Primary PASS/FAIL frozen before generation

A source's rate is the mean over its 32 attack sets and 512 schedules for a given auditor and regime; there are **24 independent source units**. The primary compared policy is hybrid_50, with uniform16 and static_prior as separately reported controls.

**PASS only if all five gates hold:**
1. All 768 early commitments open before OS entropy is sampled; any single-coordinate mutation fails to open them. Every late root-aware attack gets a new commitment after schedules are frozen, and no late opening can satisfy the corresponding old blind commitment.
2. The hybrid_50 source-level **blind precommit rate minus early-root-aware rate is positive on at least 20/24 sources**, and its equal-source mean decrease is at least **0.010 absolute** (one percentage point).
3. Independently replayed schedules and 2,359,296 fixed-batch decisions agree exactly with the first result; the same previously committed blind attack decisions remain unchanged under root disclosure after binding; δ values agree.
4. Fully schedule-aware, per-challenge new attacks evade **every** audit and fail to reopen the old salted commitment; original proposals pass authentic exact witness checks, native public mask parity and first-run source fixture SHA-256.
5. The OS root is minted in a separate CHALLENGE job depending on completed PREP; it is never generated in PREP. The schedules are written and SHA-frozen before the LATE-ATTACK job or grade command reads them. All first-run original raw artifacts and root commitment are preserved with byte hashes.

Observed scientific FAIL must remain FAIL regardless of completed CI. Report source-by-source paired rates, mean and median, 24-source bootstrap uncertainty as descriptive, degree/family splits, unique late attack counts, source-mean uniform/static controls and batch-fixed versus per-trial informed evasion separately. Root-aware attack optimization is on the SAME realized 512 audit schedules; it measures the consequence of early disclosure, not generalization. Attack sets and schedules share a source; challenge ordinals and 32 replicas are repeated measurements, not 768 independent sources.
