# RRC-23: measured scaling of learned routing and exact symbolic verification

**Executed November 10, 2026, exploratory CPU study.** Existing RRC-19 predictors, RRC-21 calibration, the RRC-18 finite-degree polynomial quotient and the public three-token interpreter were frozen. This experiment extends the actual exact-verification horizon, supplies an additional four-token interpreter, and compares complete search with the existing selective router. It trains **zero new model weights**. A single CPU host and new deterministic instance seeds support the results below; this is a Python implementation benchmark.

## Primary result: longer coefficients with the original grammar

The 84-case test crosses seven source generators with four graph topologies and three examples per cell. All model observations remain the **same degree-12 input width**, while exact integer checks use more coefficients. Complete certified class search accepts all 84 prefixes at every degree; selective routing accepts 81. A valid finite prefix does not necessarily select the future-correct program, especially at short horizons.

| Observed degree | Symbolic future correct /84 | Selective future correct /84 | Progressive symbolic median ms | Progressive selective median ms | Complete symbolic direct median ms |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 82 | 79 | **0.257** | 0.778 | 0.286 |
| 24 | 80 | 77 | **0.312** | 0.889 | 0.424 |
| 48 | 79 | 76 | **0.442** | 1.046 | 1.151 |
| 96 | 82 | 79 | **0.952** | 1.667 | 3.759 |
| 192 | 84 | 81 | **3.302** | 4.606 | 14.920 |

Warm physical medians use two rounds and 168 case evaluations per method/horizon. The entire profile is in `results/rrc23_reference_profile.json`, with the machine-specific timing separated from the deterministic semantic receipt. The host reports AMD EPYC 9V74, five available CPUs and Python 3.13.5. The 84 cases include eight intentional degree-12 visible-prefix aliases generated by the high-degree-onset family; source streams and hidden futures differ.

**Measured result:** progressive complete symbolic verification is faster at all five main horizons. Its advantage over selective progressive remains even when the arithmetic grows, since a candidate that fails degree four or twelve avoids the expensive degree-T graph execution. A complete stagewise symbolic search also retains the three additional valid-program recoveries missed by the selective route.

At degree 192, the symbolic progressive arm performs 1,018 distinct candidate checks and 2,956 paid demonstration executions (including short stages), with a nominal integer-work proxy of 8,386,548. Selective progressive performs 937 candidate checks and 2,780 paid demonstration executions, using 8,117,918 nominal work units. This saves only **3.2%** of the counted integer work, while adding OOD detection, frozen neural inference for selected cases and program ordering. Stagewise symbolic work is approximately **78.4% lower** than the direct full-horizon symbolic-work proxy on these same cases.

The 64-syntax exhaustive control also accepts 84/84 at each horizon; its future-correct result can differ from the class ordering at smaller T. The original class identities are certified for **degree 12 only**. On 600 independent representative/member comparisons at each main longer horizon, the existing groupings show zero disagreements. These are empirical checks and do not extend the formal certificate to all degrees. Differences in future accuracy come from *distinct matching programs selected by different orderings*, not a discovered universal split of one certified class.

## Independent degree-384 stress

Eight new cases, two per wiring, were generated through degree 408. At degree 384, every method finds a prefix program predicting all eight query continuations correctly. This is a smaller post-primary physical stress rather than the same 84-case cohort.

| Method | Median warm ms/case, 8 cases | Accepted/8 | Exact candidate queries |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Progressive certified symbolic | **17.209** | 8 | 80 |
| Progressive selective deferral | 21.209 | 8 | 74 |
| Direct complete certified symbolic | 42.601 | 8 | 80 |
| Direct selective deferral | 25.910 | 8 | 74 |
| Full 64-syntax search | 72.162 | 8 | 166 |

The route can save exact verifier calls but remains slower when compared against **progressive** full symbolic search. There is no observed crossover through this stress horizon. The increase in coefficient size and exact integer work comes with a higher long-prefix runtime; the verifier's early rejection remains the decisive optimization.

## Larger grammar: four unary-operation slots

A new public pre-operation \(u\in\{I,N,R,S\}\) is composed with the original three-token graph, expanding its syntax from 64 to **256 spellings**. The frozen neural model learned only the original three-slot classes; the four-way expansion is hand-coded. All 40 generated cases are accepted by complete 256-syntax search and by a fourfold copy of the old certified class ordering at every measured degree. Since no exact symbolic quotient was compiled for the four-slot language, the 256-syntax baseline is the complete finite-class reference.

| Observed degree | 4×old-class accepted /40 | Frozen neural 12 checks accepted /40 | Selective expanded accepted /40 | Full syntax 256 accepted /40 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 40 | 19 | 40 | 40 |
| 48 | 40 | 18 | 40 | 40 |
| 96 | 40 | 18 | 40 | 40 |

At degree 48, the neural twelve-candidate expansion recovers only 18/40 future outputs, and the old-class/deferral methods recover 40/40. The 256-syntax control also recovers all 40. The old degree-12 selector does not learn the new prefix-operation semantics through this expansion.

Physical timings use a **separate eight-case subset**, one round and warm caches, because exhaustive four-token execution at degree 96 is much heavier:

| Degree | Expanded symbolic median ms | Expanded selective median ms | Frozen neural 12-check median ms | Full 256-syntax median ms |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | **0.234** | 1.122 | 8.101 | 0.450 |
| 48 | **0.756** | 1.592 | 8.633 | 2.185 |

The complete 40-case correctness roster, the smaller eight-case physical timing roster and the 84-case horizon roster are distinct statistical units. The larger grammar increases the number of possible candidate checks without yielding a measured gain from the frozen neural route.

These are one-time setup costs and are excluded from the warm per-case medians. The exact symbolic class compiler is shared by both routes; no new model training occurred.

## Exact-replay evidence and decision

`bench23.py --check` regenerates the complete deterministic source-locked receipt, with RRC-22 parent receipt SHA-256, immutable model/calibration file hashes, per-arm candidate-trace digests, per-stage demonstration counts and arithmetic-work proxies. Full finite exactness and future accuracy are graded separately. The 17 dedicated RRC-23 tests audit interpreter parity, staged execution counts, five horizon cuts, expanded grammar coverage, future-collision invariance, strict candidate budgets, frozen checkpoint identities, physical-profile schemas and degree-384 reachability. Existing RRC-21/RRC-22 regressions remain separately runnable.

**Decision:** Under this CPU implementation, use progressive **complete** certified class search for the original bounded grammar. Its accuracy and speed dominate the existing selective route throughout the tested horizon. At larger syntax sizes, a class-level compiler and early rejection remain valuable baselines. A new neural training run would need either a more expressive unfamiliar grammar, a substantially cheaper compiled inference kernel, or a workload where most incorrect symbolic candidates survive long enough to make repeated exact checking expensive.
