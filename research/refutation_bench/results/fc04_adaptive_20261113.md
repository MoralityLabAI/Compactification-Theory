## Validation-only stopping threshold

Both models were **11,521-parameter** LR-01 `TypedLoop` networks trained
at four passes, 350 AdamW steps on FC-02 train only. After weights
were written to hashed checkpoints, the fixed grid
`[0.55, 0.70, 0.85, 0.93, 0.98]` was scored on **FC-02 development
alone** using the preregistered correctness/risk/recurrent-cost utility.
Chosen thresholds were **0.70 for seed 17** and **0.55 for seed 29**.

The adaptive runner evaluates passes 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and genuinely stops
executing the LR-01 cell once it obtains either a **verified, unmodified
complete Boolean countermodel** or a sufficiently stable provisional
positive prediction at two consecutive checkpoints. Otherwise it abstains
at its limit. The checker is external to the model and its CPU cost is
charged separately. A provisional positive statement has **not**
received a kernel certificate.

The registered pass/fail test required at least fixed12 certified
recall, lower mean recurrent calls, no verified false counterexamples,
and no higher provisional-positive error rate, for **both** seeds and
**both** splits. **All four comparisons passed.**

This is a specific comparison, with an important baseline pathology:
**fixed12 seed17 collapsed to 0/96 verified refutations and 96/96
wrong provisional-positive judgments** on both split types. On the
same-depth seed17 split, **fixed4** is more cell-efficient than
adaptive (117.19 vs 93.05 certified refutations per 1,000 calls),
although it finds one fewer certificate. The adaptive policy therefore
does not dominate every fixed policy or every efficiency criterion.

For seed29, fixed12 certifies all invalid examples while spending 12
passes. Adaptive certifies the same number with fewer actual calls,
and sacrifices some provisional valid coverage on deep cases.
The measured time includes the independent exact checker. Those
single-CI-run CPU timing values are hardware-dependent and not
controlled cross-device performance measurements.

## What the experiment establishes

1. Validation-calibrated stopping can reduce recurrent compute while
   retaining or increasing *exact certified counterexample recall*
   relative to a fixed maximum-depth inference policy, in this public
   finite Horn-logic suite.
2. A verifier gate protects the formal refutation output: only an
   assignment satisfying all premises and falsifying the formula is
   released. It does not certify positive claims.
3. The recurrent state is nonmonotone under extra passes. Intermediate
   passes can be more useful than the maximum; halting is consequently
   meaningful for certificate acquisition.
4. No proof of an open mathematical conjecture is claimed. These are
   synthetic propositional graphs with an already available exact
   100%-correct symbolic solver; language-to-formal translation and
   full proof synthesis are separate unfinished tasks.

The result is a successful preregistered **efficiency** finding, with
remaining provisional risk and a strong comparator-specific effect.
Confirmatory deployment would require a new independently frozen
test corpus and a verifier-backed positive proof channel.
