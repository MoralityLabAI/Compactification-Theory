# RB-CG-06: native bounded arithmetic witness generation

## Question
CG-05 produced 24 native original-source correspondence cases but did not train a model. CG-06 trains a small tied arithmetic critic to propose concrete counterexamples to altered advertisements. The original mathematical theorems remain unchanged. The actor consumes an explicit, hand-authored arithmetic type adapter verified against the selected original declaration; it is not a raw arbitrary Lean parser. The problem is finite because each advertised theorem quantifies over explicitly bounded Fin coordinates, mapped to original Nat/Int values. A successful counterexample refutes that bounded advertised proposition, and a positive finite proof certifies that same scope. No conclusion about unbounded mathematical claims follows.

## Policy and controls
The fixed proposal grammar contains PROVE (finite decidable advertised proposition) and concrete tuples of Fin coordinate indices. No reference proof, source theorem name, split, omission label, or truth value of a retained atom is exposed to the learner. For each proposed tuple, serialize the retained arithmetic expressions and conclusion as typed prefix operators and numeric ground operands, with boundaries. A shared GRU scores whether that tuple is a counterexample; this is a learned ground-expression evaluator. It does not read the original raw module. Train only on train-root tuple labels from the exact finite evaluator, two seeds 111/113, widths 32 and 64, 12 epochs fixed, AdamW .003, batch 128, weighted binary cross entropy with fixed positive-class weight computed on train. All tuples are scored at test; rank witnesses by logits and PROVE by the negative maximum witness logit. Model-generated certificates consist of the actual chosen tuple or a finite proof action. A proposed false accusation on a true advertised statement is logged even when rejected. The deterministic finite precheck rejects bad proposals before native replay; a proposal budget is not a native invocation or matched FLOP budget. Record both candidate evaluations and actual proof replay counts.

Compare prove-first lexical, witness-first lexical, seeded random, center-out (coordinate indices near midpoint), and an exact finite arithmetic evaluator over the same pool. The last is a strong full-information symbolic baseline and should saturate at one proposal. Report complete verified certificates at budgets 1/2/4/8, supported and refuted coverage, rejected proposals, false-accusation attempts, model parameter counts, CPU time and candidate evaluations. Independently compile all distinct accepted certificates (including those from baselines) and print axiom closure; reject any unexpected dependency or failed proof. No claim of neural superiority over exact solving. Primary feasibility gate for both seeds of width32: at least 85% complete held-out certificates at 4 proposals and at most 10% first-proposal false accusations on supported cases. Width64 secondary. Preserve first results, including failures. Next campaign should introduce partially observed theorem definitions or unknown semantic bridges if exact arithmetic remains dominant.
