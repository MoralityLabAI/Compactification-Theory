# Cyclic addition: an exact closure benchmark and an empirical warning test

Reference: Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith & Steinhardt, [*Progress Measures for Grokking via Mechanistic Interpretability*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217) (ICLR 2023), §§3–5. Their mainline one-layer transformer uses addition mod 113, 30% training pairs, 40,000 full-batch epochs, and finds Fourier multiplication and *restricted*/*excluded* logit losses that move before test accuracy. Our small CPU study is **not a replication** of its model or its reported grokking curves. EvalMath's general intervention-preserving quotient remains canonical; this finite cyclic example is a companion benchmark in PR #28's scope.

## Exact result

Let p be prime, X=Z_p×Z_p, h(a,b)=a+b mod p and T_(u,v)(a,b)=(a+u,b+v). All sums and shifts are modulo p. Let χ_j(s)=exp(2πijs/p), 1≤j<p. Its real code C_j(a,b)=(Re χ_j(h(a,b)), Im χ_j(h(a,b))) satisfies

    C_j(T_(u,v)(a,b)) = Rot(2πj(u+v)/p) C_j(a,b).       (13)

**Theorem (minimal quotient and invariant fixed-target risk).** For the task h and any alphabet of additive shifts (including the empty word), the all-word task-equivalence quotient consists of exactly p classes indexed by s=a+b. It is an autonomous task-preserving quotient; any exact finite state code needs at least ceil(log₂ p) fixed-length bits. Every nontrivial phase C_j is faithful on these classes and implements shifts by rotations. For any deterministic code C:X→Z and *any* state measure μ, define Y_w=e_{h(T_w(X))}∈R^p using the standard one-hot metric, and R_w(C)=inf_g E_μ||Y_w-g(C(X))||₂². Then **R_w(C)=R_empty(C)** for every additive word w. Consequently every nested code path has the same fixed-target threshold k* for every such word and for their max at any horizon.

*Proof.* Equality of sums implies equal outputs after every shift, since h(T_w(a,b))=h(a,b)+c_w. Conversely the empty word distinguishes unequal sums. Thus the quotient is precisely h and has p classes, giving the bit bound. χ_j(s+c)=χ_j(c)χ_j(s), which is the real rotation (13). Since p is prime and j≠0 mod p, χ_j(s)=χ_j(s') implies s=s' mod p, so C_j is faithful. Finally, Y_w=P_(c_w)Y_empty, where P_(c_w) is the orthogonal permutation matrix shifting one-hot coordinates. Conditional expectation commutes with fixed linear P_(c_w), and the squared Euclidean norm is permutation-invariant: E||Y_w-E[Y_w|C]||²=E||Y_empty-E[Y_empty|C]||². This holds for any μ and C, including learned, quantized codes. Taking maxima and threshold crossings preserves equality. □

**What it rules out.** For this equivariant task and one-hot loss, merely adding additive interventions to the fixed-target Bayes-risk diagnostic cannot supply a new warning beyond present-label recoverability. This does *not* prove that learned hidden states are closed under shifts, nor that their compressed transition is accurate. It also does not make Fourier restricted/excluded *cross-entropy* equal to Bayes squared-error risk. Numeric scalar targets 0,…,p−1 are not isometric under cyclic label shifts, so their MSE can misleadingly change with w. For unquantized finite-state codes an arbitrary generic one-dimensional real projection can be injective; a nontrivial dimensional threshold therefore needs specified finite precision or a restricted decoder. One character pair suffices on prime p in exact arithmetic but carries at least log₂ p bits of distinguishability and may require a complex label decoder. Replacing the pair by one arbitrary real coordinate can also remain injective without a precision bound. The experiment must not claim a novel information-loss phase transition based solely on dimension.

The character identity χ_j(a+b)=χ_j(a)χ_j(b) is precisely the rotation/product mechanism underlying Nanda et al.'s Fourier multiplication. The unit tests enumerate exact quotient and conditional risks for p≤7 and check numerical rotations separately; they do not certify floating-point phase identities.

## Predeclared CPU checkpoint test

`modular_checkpoint.py` runs a deliberately smaller one-layer ReLU causal transformer (p=31, embedding width 32, two attention heads, MLP width 64, no LayerNorm or tied embeddings), AdamW lr 0.001, weight decay 1, full batch, 6,000 epochs. Each independent seed draws 288 of 961 input pairs for training (30% rounded), evaluating the other 673. Calibration seed 91 selects the three largest diagonal input-logit Fourier frequencies from its *final* checkpoint without test labels; that fixed selection is used on confirmation seeds 0 and 1. Calibration curves are descriptive, not independent predictive evidence. No frequency is selected or changed based on confirmation outcomes. Checkpoints are fixed at epochs 0, 100, 250, 500, 1000, 1500, 2000, 3000, 4000, 5000, and 6000.

At each confirmation checkpoint, take a 2D DFT of all p² model logits along the input indices as Nanda et al. do. *Restricted loss* keeps DC and the (j,j),(-j,-j) terms of the three calibration-selected frequencies; *excluded loss* removes those terms from the original logits. Both are cross-entropies, on held-out and training pairs respectively. Compare restricted loss with three different nonselected diagonal frequencies of the same dimension, and with the full diagonal projection. The extra spectral controls prevent labeling any improvement from a generic Fourier filter as a discovered sparse circuit.

For compression, measure the model's final-token **MLP ReLU activations**, not its output logits. Fit a PCA basis on training pairs alone; compare a fixed-seed random orthonormal channel projection. Take nested prefixes of k=16,8,4,2,1,0 coordinates, center by training mean and quantize each coordinate to signed 8 bits at training scale ±3 standard deviations (clipping outliers). The 0-coordinate code is constant. Compute the exact empirical *one-hot Bayes* squared-error risk on training pairs for the fixed future target e_((a+b+1) mod p) from the quantized codes; threshold δ=0.1. This unrestricted fiber decoder may have lookup complexity proportional to attained codes. As a controlled-decoder diagnostic, fit a 31-output affine ridge readout on training pairs with **17 slots at every k** (one intercept, 16 coordinate slots, unused slots zero), ridge 0.001, and report its penalized training objective and held-out squared error. Its threshold is δ=0.5 on that penalized objective; it is not the Bayes risk and should be named separately. PCA axes, quantizer scales, and readout weights are refit at each epoch from the training pairs only; this is part of the measurement procedure and can itself cause movement of k*(t). Fixed code precision and decoder class do not fix the total number of active code bits, which is 8k.

**Prospective descriptive decision rule, frozen before results.** Call a run a delayed-generalization instance only if a checkpoint has training accuracy ≥0.95 and held-out accuracy ≤0.5 before a later checkpoint reaches held-out accuracy ≥0.9. The recoverability warnings are first checkpoints where the Bayes threshold index along [16,8,4,2,1,0] is at least 4 (two coordinates survive), or the affine-objective index is at least 2 (eight survive). Compare their first-warning checkpoints with restricted test cross-entropy falling below 0.75 log(p), and excluded train cross-entropy exceeding ordinary train cross-entropy by 0.5. A warning counts as advance only if it precedes the first ≥0.9 held-out checkpoint by at least one sampled checkpoint. The question 'beyond Nanda et al.' asks whether it warns earlier and more consistently than *both* Fourier measures across the two confirmation seeds. These cutoffs are heuristic, not calibrated confidence thresholds; false alarms and missing warnings must be reported. If delayed generalization does not occur, the CPU study cannot test advance warning. Scanning held-out restricted loss as a progress metric is conventional here, but it is not a blind deployment forecast.

This is CPU-only in a Torch-enabled image, with no accelerator requested, no network data, and no model API. Source and output receipts identify exact versions and seeds. The prospective rule was fixed before reading the confirmation output. Claims are limited by two confirmation seeds, checkpoint spacing, smaller p and architecture than Nanda et al., and the ground-truth equivalence of all additive future one-hot risks proved above.

## Exploratory longer-run addendum, fixed before new seeds

The completed 6,000-epoch run [exp:exp_01m6aw5afae7dtp2ezj2nkrekd] did **not** have a delayed held-out generalization event in either confirmation seed. Training accuracy reached 1.0, but at epoch 6000 held-out accuracies were 0.07727 and 0.07132; the calibration seed reached 0.2140. The Bayes threshold index was 4 already at initialization for both PCA and random projections, while the fixed-affine threshold index remained 0; hence the initial CPU envelope cannot establish an advance warning. The selected frequencies (15,6,12) came from the independent calibration seed 91.

After seeing that result, we chose an explicitly **exploratory** duration extension: new seeds 2 and 3, up to epoch 20000, retaining the same train fraction, architecture, losses, quantization, decoders, alarm cutoffs, and **frozen** calibration frequencies (15,6,12). Additional observation epochs are 8000, 10000, 12000, 15000, 20000. A two-step execution smoke for seeds 2 and 3 checked only output structure; no accuracy or risk values from those seeds were inspected before fixing this addendum. The new duration was not part of the original preregistration and is not independent confirmation of a duration choice. If these models also do not grok, stop rather than retune cutoffs to create a positive claim. [The final results](CYCLIC_GROKKING_RESULTS.md) report exactly that outcome and distinguish structural proof, measured thresholds, and the untested advance-warning claim.
