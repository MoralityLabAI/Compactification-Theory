# IDQ-01: Ingression as a testable quotient of controlled dynamics

**Status:** mathematical research note, 2026-11-04. The deterministic theorem and finite-horizon stochastic bound below have elementary proofs. The companion Python tests check only specified finite examples. There are **no biological or model measurements**. This is MoralityLabAI's mathematical extension, not a theorem claimed by Michael Levin.

## 1. Exact controlled quotient (IDQ-01/T1)

Let S and Z be sets, A an action set, h:S→Z a surjection, F_a:S→S microscopic update maps, and G_a:Z→Z macroscopic updates. Assume for **every** s and a:

    h(F_a(s)) = G_a(h(s)).                                          (1)

Fix a finite horizon H. A feedback policy pi_t may depend on the *entire observed macro-history* z_0,...,z_t, and can be randomized using a shared external random tape.

**Theorem T1.** If the micro-controller sees only h(s_t), then for identical macro starting state and identical policy random tape, its projected trajectory equals the trajectory generated by G under the same policy at every time t≤H.

**Proof.** At time zero z_0=h(s_0). Suppose the projected trajectories agree through t. The feedback policies then choose identical a_t. Equation (1) gives h(s_{t+1})=h(F_{a_t}(s_t))=G_{a_t}(h(s_t))=z_{t+1}. Induct. QED.

**Corollaries.**
1. For any target B⊆Z, the first hitting times of h^{-1}(B) and B agree pathwise.
2. If perturbations P_b:S→S and Q_b:Z→Z also satisfy h∘P_b=Q_b∘h, every interleaved intervention / update schedule commutes. Hence recovery after *specified* disturbances is exactly transportable.
3. If h is a bijection, this is a conjugacy of controlled maps. If h is many-to-one it is a semiconjugacy and the macro dynamics are a quotient; a topological **homeomorphism** is needed for topological conjugacy on topological spaces.

The universal quantifier in (1) is essential. Agreement on one rollout or with one initial state does not identify a macro law.

**Fibre obstruction.** With S={0,1,2}, h(0)=h(1)=0 and h(2)=1, choose F(0)=2 and F(1)=1. There is no G with (1): the same macro-state 0 would have to transition to both 1 and 0. The companion test exhibits this impossibility explicitly.

## 2. Approximate stochastic quotient (IDQ-01/T2)

Let S,Z,A be finite. Let K_a(s,·) be stochastic microscopic transition kernels and L_a(z,·) macroscopic kernels. Let h:S→Z and push forward K to Z. Assume a **uniform**, actionwise bound

    TV( h_* K_a(s,·), L_a(h(s),·) ) ≤ epsilon                     (2)

for all micro-states and actions. Let micro rewards r(s,a)∈[0,1] and macro rewards R(z,a)∈[0,1], with

    |r(s,a) - R(h(s),a)| ≤ delta.                                 (3)

A macro-observation-history policy may use randomness, but both systems are coupled to use the same policy random tape while their observed histories agree. Start with z_0=h(s_0).

**Theorem T2 (finite-horizon return transfer).** For any such common policy and horizon H≥1,

    |E[sum_{t=0}^{H-1} r(s_t,a_t)]
     - E[sum_{t=0}^{H-1} R(z_t,a_t)]|
       ≤ H*delta + epsilon*H*(H-1)/2.                            (4)

**Proof.** Condition on paths still matched in macro-state at time t. Maximal coupling under (2) extends the match for one step with probability at least 1-epsilon. By the union bound, disagreement at t has probability at most t*epsilon. When matched, reward differences are at most delta by (3); when unmatched, both rewards lie in [0,1], so the difference is at most 1. The absolute expectation difference at step t is bounded by delta+t*epsilon. Sum t=0,...,H-1. QED.

For a target-hit event by time H, the same coupling yields a probability discrepancy at most min(1,H*epsilon), *provided* both target sets correspond through h and the event includes a consistently defined observation endpoint. This concerns the same policy on the two systems; optimization over different unrestricted micro-information policies is a different contract.

**Finite exact witness.** Take S=Z={0,1}, h=id, micro K(0,1)=1/4, K(0,0)=3/4, K(1,1)=1; macro L(0,0)=1, L(1,1)=1; reward r=R=state, H=3. Then E_microscopic return=11/16, E_macroscopic return=0, epsilon=1/4, delta=0, and the bound is 3/4. Python Fraction arithmetic verifies the numbers.

## 3. Operational agency and pre-embodiment signatures

For a bounded target B and perturbation family D, define **recovery competence** for a specified policy pi by

    C_H(pi,s;B,D) = min_{d∈D} P_pi(hit h^{-1}(B) within H | d(s)).

This is a declared functional criterion, **not** a measurement of experience, personhood, soul, or causal efficacy of nonphysical objects. Compare against matched controls: randomized couplings, shuffled stimulus histories, identical action budgets, and predictors frozen *before* evaluation. A pre-target predictor may track change in C_H or in condition (2), but good prediction of later organization alone does not imply backwards causation.

A "free lunch" score is meaningful only relative to a preregistered predictive model M and an explicit accounted resource budget (evolutionary prior, wiring, training, state and search costs). One proposed diagnostic is

    Surprise_M(y | a,s) = -log M(y | a,s),

reported alongside calibration and negative controls. Surprise under a misspecified baseline cannot establish nonphysical intervention.
