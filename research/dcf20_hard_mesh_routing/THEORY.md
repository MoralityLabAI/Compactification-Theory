# DCF-20 theory: when does routing pay?

Write `ΔU(q) = EU_exact(q) − EU_bp(q)` for a query, and `c_exact`, `c_bp` for the per-query compute charges (λ times modelled kernel ms, with launch overhead shared across the batch).

**Epoch routing.** It chooses one operator per batch-epoch, so its value is `Σ_epochs max(0, Σ_q ΔU(q) − Δc)` minus the better fixed operator's value. It is positive only when the sign of `Σ_q ΔU − Δc` changes between epochs. If BP is nearly right, the sum is near zero and the choice hardly matters. If BP is often wrong, the sum is large and positive in every epoch, so fixed exact is already optimal. The DCF-20 scan sees exactly this: the headroom is at most .00006 in every world with a decision-change rate above 3%.

**Query routing.** It escalates a subset S of each step's queries: BP for all, exact for S. Its value is `Σ_{q∈S} ΔU(q) − c_exact(S) − a_exact·[S ≠ ∅]` relative to fixed BP. Against fixed exact it saves `c_exact(complement of S)` and loses `Σ_{q∉S} ΔU(q)`. It wins when the flag that defines S has high recall for queries with ΔU > 0, and when the compute saved on the rest is worth more than the utility those misses cost.

**Why abstention is the right flag here.** For an aligned query `π = x_i ⊕ x_j` with i and j in different blocks, cluster BP reads the parity out from independent block beliefs: `p_bp = (1 − s_i s_j)/2` with `s = 1 − 2 b(x=1)`. A low-noise XOR sensor on {i, j} makes the true parity nearly certain. If the singleton marginals stay uncertain, though, `s_i s_j ≈ 0` and `p_bp ≈ 0.5`. BP then abstains, and exact acts with utility near 1. The failure is under-confidence, not a confident error. It sits in the middle of the abstention band, which is why near-threshold margins (DCF-19B, DCF-20 probe) miss it, while "BP abstains" catches 95% of the decision changes.

**Price dependence.** Escalation spends exact compute on every abstention, including those exact also abstains on. Against fixed exact, its advantage grows with λ (exact work saved on the 37.5% of queries BP decides) and shrinks with the utility lost on BP's rare confident errors. On these traces the crossover lies between λ = 0.03 and 0.1. Against fixed BP its advantage shrinks with λ, and at λ = 0.3 it still leads by .025. A policy that is simultaneously best against both fixed operators therefore exists only in a band of compute prices. That band should be measured, not assumed, for any deployment.
