## Observations
- p50c (the 50-pass run, the only one with a large reported gain): two sparse edits, `layers.15.mlp.down_proj` and `layers.19.mlp.down_proj`. Each delta is exactly rank one (numerical rank 1, top singular value holds 100% of the energy) on 64 changed input columns (of 3,584), with 65,260 and 64,928 of the 65,536 support entries changed in bf16 (99.6% and 99.1%). It fits `dW = -c u (u^T W_S)` with c = 6.0002 and 6.0003 and relative residual 0.23% and 0.29% (bf16 rounding). This is the workorder's `dW = -6u(mask * u^T W)` form, so it amplifies and reverses the targeted projection to -5 times its original component on the support. The two layers' directions u have cosine 0.849.
- p10c: 30 tensors (attention `out_proj` / `o_proj` and MLP `down_proj` in layers 8-22), stored low-rank; each fits `dW = -c u (u^T W)` with c between 1.4992 and 1.5003 and residual 2.7-4.7%, which is consistent with a rank-one update on the full matrix rounded to bf16. After rounding, the numerical rank at tolerance 1e-3 is 271-717 even though the top singular direction holds at least 99.78% of the energy: bf16 storage turns an algebraic rank-one edit into a numerically full-rank delta. Total changed entries 72,986,982.
- p25c: zeroes 256 columns of `layers.15.self_attn.o_proj` (one attention head); the delta has rank 256 and is not low rank.
- Hash and structure checks establish that the published edits are what the logs say. They do not by themselves show why p50c improves math; that needs the generation measurements.

## Hypotheses (not tested here)
p50c works because `u` is a loop-or-repetition direction that these two MLPs write, so removing it breaks decoding loops. `phase_e3.py` tests the analytic version of that idea: estimate `u` from the model's own looping generations, localize layers causally, and compare the published-form edit against minimum-energy edits and random controls.
