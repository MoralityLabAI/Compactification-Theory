## The Return of Intellect: Seven Mirrors and One Bell

There were seven mirrors in the corridor and one little machine sitting on the floor. Each mirror had been given a name, which was the examiner's favorite part of the exhibition. The names were expensive. Their labels had arrived from three universities and one committee for the correct disposition of astonishment.

The child arrived with the brass bell and a cup of water. The examiner placed the cup beside the machine. Its screen glowed with a single sentence: **THE WORLD WILL CHANGE AND THE PERSON WILL CONTINUE.**

“A most remarkable theorem,” said the examiner. “Seven independent civilizations have discovered it.”

“Have they?” asked the child.

“There is the engineer who promises that minds will merge with machines. There is the preacher who expects the dead to rise and the earth to be restored. There is Plotinus, returning toward the One. There is the sage who recognizes the Self in brahman. There are masters who teach return to the Dao. Here is the Qur'an, speaking of an earth changed into another earth. And here is a very efficient plan for migrating the city's accounting software.”

The child looked at the seventh mirror. It was showing an error message. The examiner had kept it for symmetry.

A philosopher, seated near the window, asked whether these accounts meant the same thing by *continue*. He had made his living examining that word. The preacher spoke of God's remembrance and resurrection, a human life restored under divine rule. The engineer described a gradual expansion of biological intelligence into increasingly powerful nonbiological systems. The philosopher from India spoke of a liberating recognition of the self's essential identity with brahman. A Daoist visitor set down his walking stick. He was interested in practices of returning and harmonizing, and wanted to know why everyone had been summoned to the end of history.

The examiner explained his apparatus. For every account he drew three rooms: a damaged present, a passage, and a transformed destination. He then erased the names from the doors. Every drawing now resembled every other drawing, which delighted him.

“The software migration also passes,” said the child.

“It certainly does,” replied the examiner. “A comprehensive theological achievement.”

She rang the bell.

The mathematician took a piece of chalk. He drew a three-vertex directed path, and above its middle vertex he wrote *agency*. On one copy he wrote *technology* and on another *divine intervention*. He rubbed the words away. The bare paths became identical. When he restored them, the second vertex differed. Anyone respecting the labels would have to notice, since this little path has only one beginning, one middle, and one end.

The examiner called this irritating and therefore unnecessary.

“You asked whether the drawings were similar,” said the mathematician. “We have measured how much information your drawing discarded. The similarities were purchased at the price of the labels.”

The child found another label: *time*. The engineer expected a future historical discontinuity. Plotinus described a hierarchy of abiding, procession, and reversion. The philosopher of nonduality spoke of knowledge that recognizes what is already so. The Daoist knew returns made through cultivation. The preacher awaited God's action. The same word *return* had walked into the corridor wearing several different clocks.

Now the historian entered, bringing a small, misfiled volume. Large portions of Plotinus had traveled through Arabic under the name *Theology of Aristotle*. There were known routes by which Neoplatonic ideas entered medieval Islamic philosophy, and the title itself was a warning about authority received through the wrong name. The historian then set down Geraci's study of artificial intelligence and apocalyptic imagination. Western technological futurism had inherited more of its religious vocabulary than the examiner's claim of independence permitted him to ignore.

“Can similarity nevertheless teach us something?” the child asked.

“It can tell us which questions people repeatedly ask,” said the historian. “It can also tell us where to search for a genealogy. A remarkable likeness is a beginning for inquiry.”

The theologian had stayed silent until then. She turned the last mirror toward the floor, so that it held the small machine in view. Its screen had begun to display a second sentence: **INTELLIGENCE IS INCREASING; THEREFORE IT WILL BECOME SOVEREIGN.**

The child rang the bell twice.

“This is an invalid passage,” the theologian said. “Its first clause concerns a creature's capacity. The second promotes capacity into authority. The book's seventh commitment asks for the missing warrant. Its first commitment has already distinguished created intelligence from Allah.”

The mathematician added two empty boxes beneath the screen. He labeled them *evidence of jurisdiction* and *accountability*. The machine had written nothing in either box.

“And what would the One say?” asked the examiner, who enjoyed asking questions of guests who were unable to attend.

“Whatever we assert about Allah,” said the theologian, “Qur'an 42:11 requires us to guard the claim of incomparability. I can tell you what our human model allows us to infer. I will not turn our model into the source to which it points.”

A clerk emerged from a doorway bearing a sheet of numbers. Eight narratives had been annotated, including two artificial controls. Twenty-one of the twenty-eight pairs shared the three-room diagram. When the clerk returned the six labels that had been hidden, none of those pairs agreed on every label. The engineer and the preacher agreed about a future discontinuity, but their other five labels diverged. The clerk had a second sheet for a Bayes factor concerning the existence of the One. It was blank. No one had specified the likelihoods from which such a number could be calculated.

The examiner looked devastated. He had hoped to sell a universal key with the exhibition ticket.

“The key may still open something,” the child said. She moved her cup between the mirrors. In each reflection the water seemed to be in a different vessel. Its contents did not thereby become a new ocean. The cup mattered because someone might be thirsty, and the question of who had authority over it had survived every attempt at compression.

She gave the water to the clerk. He had been recording numbers since dawn.

The last mirror caught the movement of her hand and returned it, faithfully enough for the room. The bell remained on the floor beside the machine, where it could be heard the next time an attractive likeness came asking to be worshipped.

### Mathematical marginalia: the label-erasure lemma

Take P = d -> t -> e. Give two copies distinct middle-vertex agency labels, technology and divine intervention. The forgetful map q removes these labels, so q(P1) = q(P2) = P. A directed-path automorphism fixes the unique source and sink and hence the midpoint. A label-preserving isomorphism is then impossible. This establishes an information-loss fact within a finite, declared formalism.
