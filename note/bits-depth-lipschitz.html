Bits, depth and Lipschitz constants in deep ReLU approximation

Setting: \(F\) is the unit ball of the Hölder class \(C^s([0,1]^d)\). Networks are ReLU with \(W\) weights in \([-R,R]\), depth \(L\), and \(b\) bits per weight. Error is the sup norm, worst case over \(F\).

P proved here (S = sketch) O cited, source opened R cited from memory, not opened J conjecture

Bottom line

  • Capability per bit is fundamentally bounded: \(\varepsilon \gtrsim (\text{total bits})^{-s/d}\), with no Lipschitz assumption.
  • Capability per parameter is not. The \(W^{-s/d}\) barrier holds exactly when the effective bits per weight, \(\log_2(RK/\varepsilon)\), are \(O(\log W)\). For bounded weights that is the same as polylog depth. It also holds whenever weights are chosen continuously in the target.
  • The \(W^{-2s/d}\) ceiling is real for ReLU, and it comes from VC dimension, not from any Lipschitz constant.
  • Most standard generalization assumptions exclude \(\gamma>1\) only through a polynomial bound on \(K\) or a local or \(L^2\) version of it. None excludes it through input-Lipschitzness alone.

§Notation

  • \(W\): number of weights, biases included. \(L\): depth. \(n\): maximum width. Each weight lies in \([-R,R]\), with \(R\) fixed, and takes one of \(2^b\) values.
  • \(\varepsilon^*(W,b,L) = \inf_{\text{arch},\,\theta}\ \sup_{f\in F}\|f-f_\theta\|_\infty\), the minimax error.
  • \(H(\varepsilon)=\log_2 N(\varepsilon,F,\|\cdot\|_\infty)\), the metric entropy of \(F\).
  • \(K\): the Lipschitz constant of \(\theta\mapsto f_\theta\) from \(([-R,R]^W,\ell^\infty)\) to \((C([0,1]^d),\|\cdot\|_\infty)\).
  • Rates are written \(\varepsilon = W^{-\gamma s/d}\). \(\gamma=1\) is the classical rate and \(\gamma=2\) the deep ReLU ceiling.
  • Ou–Schenkel–Bölcskei (OSB) use \(W\) for width. Their results are converted here to weight count, \(W\asymp \text{width}^2\cdot L\).

§0The four background claims

All four are correct as stated.

0.1 Continuous selection (DeVore–Howard–Micchelli)

Correct. R DHM 1989. O Restated for Hölder balls in sup norm as YZ20, Theorem 3.2(2).

P Place \(W+1\) disjoint bumps \(\varphi_j\) of width \(h\asymp W^{-1/d}\) and height 1, so that \(\|\varphi_j\|_{C^s}\lesssim h^{-s}\). The sphere \(S=\{\sum_j c_j\varphi_j : \max_j|c_j|=\rho\}\) with \(\rho\asymp h^s\asymp W^{-s/d}\) lies in \(F\).

If the weight selection \(a:F\to\mathbb R^W\) is continuous, then \(g(f)=a(f)-a(-f)\) is odd and continuous on \(S\cong S^W\). By Borsuk–Ulam, \(g(f_0)=0\) for some \(f_0\in S\).

Then \(f_0\) and \(-f_0\) receive the same network \(\phi\), so \(\max(\|f_0-\phi\|_\infty,\|{-f_0}-\phi\|_\infty)\ge\|f_0\|_\infty=\rho\). Nothing is assumed about the map from weights to functions.

0.2 The deep ReLU rate

Correct, and now known for every \(s>0\).

  • Yarotsky 2018 proved it for \(s\le 1\), stated via the modulus of continuity R, O as described in YZ20 §1.
  • YZ20, Theorem 3.3 O: for every \(s>0\) and every \(p\in(s/d,\,2s/d]\), error \(O(W^{-p})\) is achievable with depth \(L\le c\,W^{pd/s-1}\). There is no log factor, even at \(p=2s/d\).
  • YZ20, Theorem 3.2 O: \(p>2s/d\) is infeasible for any piecewise-polynomial activation. Any \(p\in(s/d,\,2s/d]\) forces \(L\ge c\,W^{pd/s-1}/\log W\).
  • YZ20 state on p. 7 that these results ignore weight magnitude and precision O.
0.3 Counting

Correct P. If every \(f\in F\) lies within \(\varepsilon\) of one of the at most \(2^{Wb}\) networks, those networks form an \(\varepsilon\)-net. Hence

\[ N(2\varepsilon,F)\le 2^{Wb}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Wb\ \ge\ H(2\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{-d/s}. \]
  • The entropy estimate is Kolmogorov–Tikhomirov 1959 R; YZ20 §7 cites it in the same form O.
  • Choosing the architecture per target adds at most \(O(W\log W)\) bits, which changes no rate.
0.4 Covering

Correct, with a slightly loose constant P. Cover the box \([-R,R]^W\) by \(\lceil RK/\varepsilon\rceil^W\) \(\ell^\infty\)-cubes of radius \(\varepsilon/K\). Their images are \(\varepsilon\)-balls, so

\[ H(2\varepsilon)\ \le\ W\log_2\!\big(1+RK/\varepsilon\big). \]

This is Petrova–Wojtaszczyk (PW), Proposition 4.4 O. Three refinements are used below.

(i) Local version. P Only flatness at the approximants matters. Suppose each \(f\) has some \(\theta_f\) with \(\|f-f_{\theta_f}\|\le\varepsilon\), and \(\|f_{\theta'}-f_{\theta_f}\|\le\varepsilon\) whenever \(\|\theta'-\theta_f\|_\infty\le\delta\). Rounding \(\theta_f\) to a grid of spacing \(2\delta\) gives \(H(4\varepsilon)\le W\log_2(1+R/\delta)\).

(ii) Bits and \(K\) are the same currency. P Rounding a \(K\)-Lipschitz network to \(b=\lceil\log_2(2RK/\varepsilon)\rceil\) bits moves it by at most \(\varepsilon/2\). Define \(b_{\rm eff}=\log_2(RK/\varepsilon)\). Both bounds then read \(W\,b_{\rm eff}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-d/s}\).

(iii) For networks, \(K\) is a depth quantity. P For \(R\ge1\),

\[ K\ \le\ L\prod_{l=1}^{L}(n_{l-1}+1)R. \]

Activations satisfy \(\max(1,\|h_l\|_\infty)\le\prod_{k\le l}(n_{k-1}+1)R\). Under a weight perturbation of size \(\delta\), the error \(E_l=\|z_l-z_l'\|_\infty\) obeys \(E_l\le n_{l-1}R\,E_{l-1}+(n_{l-1}+1)\,\delta\,\max(1,\|h_{l-1}\|_\infty)\), with \(E_0=0\). Unroll the recursion.

OSB Proposition 3.3 states the \(R=1\) case as quantization error \(\le L(\text{width}+1)^L 2^{-b}\) O.

§1Q1: precision growing as \(b=W^\alpha\)

Theorem 1

For fixed \(s,d,R\) and \(1\le\min(b,L)\le W\),

\[ c\,\max\Big\{(Wb)^{-s/d},\ (WL\log W)^{-s/d}\Big\}\ \le\ \varepsilon^*(W,b,L)\ \le\ C\Big(\frac{W\min(b,L)}{\log^{c'}W}\Big)^{-s/d}. \]

With \(b=W^\alpha\), \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), the counting rate \(W^{-(1+\alpha)s/d}\) is attained up to log factors if and only if \(L\gtrsim W^\alpha\), up to logs. Raising \(b\) beyond \(W\) gains nothing.

Lower bound. P Counting (§0.3) gives the first term. For the second, put bumps of height \(3\varepsilon\) on \(\asymp\varepsilon^{-d/s}\) grid points. Approximating every sign pattern to \(\varepsilon\) means the thresholded networks shatter the grid, so \(\varepsilon^{-d/s}\lesssim\mathrm{VCdim}\lesssim WL\log W\). The VC bound is Bartlett–Harvey–Liaw–Mehrabian 2019 R, used the same way in YZ20 Theorem 3.2(3) O.

Upper bound for \(d=s=1\). O OSB Theorem 5.1 proves it, with weights in \((-4,4)\cap2^{-b}\mathbb Z\), width \(w\), and \(W\asymp w^2L\). There are three regimes:

  • Proper quantization, \(E_2\log w/L\le b<E_1L\log w\): \(\varepsilon^*\asymp(w^2Lb)^{-1}=(Wb)^{-1}\), with matching upper and lower constants. This is the memory-optimal regime, and it answers Q1 for \(d=s=1\).
  • Over-quantization, \(b\ge E_1L\log w\): \(\varepsilon^*\asymp(w^2L^2\log w)^{-1}\), flat in \(b\).
  • Under-quantization, \(b<E_2\log w/L\): \(c\,2^{-Lb}\le\varepsilon^*\le C\,\alpha^{-Lb}\) for some \(\alpha>1\). This regime is empty once \(L\gtrsim\log w\).

OSB Proposition 4.1 O gives the depth–precision tradeoff: \(\mathcal R^{ka}_{kb}(w,L)\subseteq\mathcal R^{a}_{b}(16w,(k+2)L)\). OSB say the extensions to \(d\) dimensions and to Hölder smoothness follow readily, but they don't prove them.

Upper bound for general \((s,d)\). P·S YZ20's construction already has the right information layout: about \(W\) encoding weights carrying \(W^{pd/s-1}\) bits each (§7 and Table 1 O). With \(p=(1+\alpha)s/d\), that is exactly \(W^\alpha\) bits per weight at depth \(W^\alpha\). What it lacks is a bounded-magnitude, uniform-\(b\)-bit version. Sketch:

  1. Grid. Use \(N\asymp W\) coarse cells, each with \(B\asymp W^\alpha\) fine cells of side \(h\asymp(NB)^{-1/d}=W^{-(1+\alpha)/d}\). Walk the fine cells of a coarse cell along a snake path, so consecutive cells are neighbours.
  2. Code. Let \(u_i=\big(D^\beta f(x_i)/h^{\,s-|\beta|}\big)_{|\beta|<s}\) be the scaled Taylor data. Taylor's theorem gives \(u_{i+1}=Tu_i+r_i\), where \(T\) is a fixed unipotent matrix and \(\|r_i\|_\infty\le C\). The decoder runs \(\tilde u_{i+1}=T\tilde u_i+c_i\), with integers \(c_i=-\mathrm{round}(Te_i+r_i)\) and \(e_i=\tilde u_i-u_i\). This keeps \(\|e_i\|_\infty\le\tfrac12\) and \(|c_i|\le\tfrac12\|T\|_\infty+C+1\), a fixed alphabet. The local Taylor polynomial built from \(\tilde u_i\) has error \(O(h^s)\). This is the sequential Kolmogorov–Tikhomirov encoding that YZ20 use O.
  3. Storage. One coarse cell needs \(O(B)+O(\log W)\) bits, so one weight with \(b\asymp W^\alpha\) bits per cell. Store bits as redundant base-4 digits, \(y=\sum_k(1+\beta_k)4^{-k}\). The first digit puts \(y\in[\tfrac13,\tfrac5{12}]\) or \(y\in[\tfrac7{12},\tfrac23]\), a gap of \(\tfrac16\). A ramp of slope 6 reads it, and \(y\leftarrow4y-(1+\beta_1)\in[\tfrac13,\tfrac23]\) shifts to the next digit, so every extraction weight is at most 6.
  4. Decoder. Three parts:
    • a lookup of the cell's encoding weight, \(O(N)\) weights;
    • a chain of width \(O(1)\) and depth \(O(B)\) that runs the recursion in the bounded variable \(h^s\tilde u\) and captures the index \(i(x)\);
    • Yarotsky's approximate multiplication, \(O(\log W)\) weights.
    Regions near cell boundaries are handled by partition of unity or by the median of shifted copies, as in YZ20 and Lu–Shen–Yang–Zhang 2021 R. This costs a constant factor.
  5. Lemma M (magnitude). P A weight \(w=2^m w'\) with \(|w'|\le1\) equals a chain of \(m\) doublings through identity units \(x=\sigma(x)-\sigma(-x)\). This costs \(O(m)\) weights and depth. Here \(m=O(\log W)\), and only the shallow lookup part needs it.
  6. Precision of the fixed weights. Round \(T\) to \(\tilde T\) with \(O(\log W)\) bits and let the encoder use \(\tilde T\). The extra term \((\tilde T-T)u_i\) stays \(O(1)\) once \(2^{-b'}h^{-s}=O(1)\). For \(b<C\log W\), apply OSB's depth–precision tradeoff, which costs a log factor in \(W\).

Total: \(O(W\log W)\) weights, depth \(O(W^\alpha+\log^2W)\), error \(O(W^{-(1+\alpha)s/d})\). I have not found this bounded-weight, \(b\)-bit statement for general \((s,d)\) in print; every ingredient is in YZ20 and OSB.

Obstruction. There is none for \(\alpha\le1\) apart from depth, which must grow like \(W^\alpha/\log W\). For \(\alpha>1\) the VC term dominates.

Memorization analogue. R Vardi–Yehudai–Shamir (ICLR 2022) show that \(\tilde\Theta(N/B)\) parameters are necessary and sufficient to memorize \(N\) points with \(B\)-bit weights, for \(B\lesssim\sqrt N\).

§2Q2: the frontier in \(K\)

Theorem 2

(a) Necessity. P, equivalent to PW Proposition 4.4 O. If error \(\varepsilon=W^{-\gamma s/d}\) is reached inside a box where \(\theta\mapsto f_\theta\) is \(K\)-Lipschitz, then

\[ \log_2(RK)\ \ge\ c\,W^{\gamma-1}-\tfrac{\gamma s}{d}\log_2W-O(1). \]

The proof substitutes \(\varepsilon\) into \(c(2\varepsilon)^{-d/s}\le H(2\varepsilon)\le W\log_2(1+RK/\varepsilon)\). Your derivation is right. At \(\gamma=1\) the condition is vacuous.

(b) Exactly tight for abstract Lipschitz parameterizations, for every \(\gamma\ge1\). O PW Theorem 4.2: the \(2^k\mathrm{rad}(F)\)-Lipschitz width with \(n\) parameters satisfies \(d_n^{\,2^k\mathrm{rad}(F)}(F)\le\varepsilon_{kn}(F)\asymp(kn)^{-s/d}\). Taking \(k=W^{\gamma-1}\) gives \(W^{-\gamma s/d}\) with \(\log K\asymp W^{\gamma-1}\). For general Lipschitz maps there is no ceiling at \(\gamma=2\).

(c) Tight for ReLU on \(1<\gamma\le2\), up to one log factor in \(\log K\). P·S In the Theorem 1 network with \(\alpha=\gamma-1\), the deep part has width \(O(1)\), weights \(O(1)\) and depth \(O(W^{\gamma-1})\), and the shallow part has depth \(O(\log W)\) and width at most \(W\). By §0.4(iii), \(\log K=O(W^{\gamma-1}+\log^2W)\).

(d) For ReLU, \(\gamma>2\) is impossible at any \(K\). This follows from the VC bound in §0.2. Above \(\gamma=2\) the \(K\)-condition is necessary but not sufficient, and the cap is combinatorial. Periodic activations remove it: YZ20 Theorem 6.1 gives error \(\exp(-c\sqrt W)\) O, consistent with (b).

(e) For bounded-weight ReLU networks, \(K\) and depth are the same resource. P Combining (a) with §0.4(iii) gives

\[ L\ \ge\ \frac{c\,W^{\gamma-1}-O(\log W)}{\log_2\big((n+1)R\big)}. \]

This recovers YZ20's depth bound by covering instead of VC. YZ20 Appendix D gets the same from Anthony–Bartlett Theorem 14.5: with per-neuron \(\ell^1\) norm at most \(V\), \(\varepsilon\ge c\,(WL)^{-s/d}\ln^{-s/d}V\) O.

(f) The two maps are independent constraints. P DHM constrains the target-to-weights map; (a) constrains the weights-to-function map. \(\gamma>1\) needs both a discontinuous selection and a large \(K\), and neither condition implies the other.

  • Cohen–DeVore–Petrova–Wojtaszczyk 2022: when both maps are Lipschitz ("stable"), the best rate is the entropy rate R (abstract seen).
  • Petrova–Wojtaszczyk 2023: rates beyond entropy need depth tending to infinity O (abstract).

The headline question. Capability per bit is bounded for information-theoretic reasons. Capability per parameter is bounded exactly to the extent that effective bits per weight are bounded. Lipschitz assumptions on the parameterization, polylog depth, continuous selection, or fixed hardware precision all impose such a bound. At bf16 (\(b=16\)) or fp32 (\(b=32\)), counting alone forces \(\gamma\le1\) asymptotically.

§3Q3: which "Lipschitz" each assumption controls

Three notions are involved:

  • (a) Input-Lipschitz: \(\sup_x\|\nabla_x f_\theta(x)\|\).
  • (b) \(K\): weights to function, global or local, in sup norm or in \(L^2(\mu)\).
  • (c) \(\beta\): the Lipschitz constant of \(\nabla_\theta\) of a loss.

Selection continuity (§0.1) is a fourth notion that is often implicit.

Lemma R (rebalancing). P Take a bias-free ReLU network with \(\prod_l\|A_l\|_2\le\Lambda\). By positive homogeneity it equals a network with every \(\|A_l\|_2\le\Lambda^{1/L}\). On that set \(K\le L\sqrt d\,n\max(1,\Lambda)\), so \(\gamma>1\) needs \(\log\Lambda\gtrsim W^{\gamma-1}\).

Lemma G (per-example Lipschitz loss). P Suppose the loss is \(G\)-Lipschitz in \(\theta\) (in \(\ell^2\)) for every \(x\) and every label \(y\) within 1 of the network's outputs; absolute loss also qualifies. Then \(|f_\theta(x)-f_{\theta'}(x)|\le G\|\theta-\theta'\|_2\), so \(K\le G\sqrt W\).

Lemma S (smoothness over realizable targets). P Suppose \(g_{\theta_0}(\theta)=\tfrac12\|f_\theta-f_{\theta_0}\|^2_{L^2(\mu)}\) is \(\beta\)-smooth for every \(\theta_0\). Since \(g_{\theta_0}(\theta_0)=0\) and \(\nabla g_{\theta_0}(\theta_0)=0\), the descent lemma gives

\[ \|f_\theta-f_{\theta_0}\|_{L^2(\mu)}\ \le\ \sqrt\beta\,\|\theta-\theta_0\|_2 . \]
  • With \(\mu\) a point mass (per-example smoothness), this gives \(K\le\sqrt{\beta W}\) in sup norm.
  • \(L^2\) entropy has the same order \(\varepsilon^{-d/s}\) (Birman–Solomyak R), so \(\gamma>1\) needs \(\log\beta\gtrsim W^{\gamma-1}\).
  • This needs smooth activations, because ReLU networks are never globally \(\beta\)-smooth.

Lemma C (a single target constrains nothing). P Let \(f_\theta=f^*+a\sin(N\theta)g_1+a\cos(N\theta)g_2\) with \(g_1,g_2\) orthonormal in \(L^2(\mu)\). Then \(\|f_\theta-f^*\|^2=a^2\) for all \(\theta\), so \(\beta=0\), while the \(L^2\) Lipschitz constant \(aN\) can be anything.

Lemma N (lazy training). P If the trained predictor equals \(f_{\theta_0}+J(\theta-\theta_0)\) up to error \(\eta\), then the worst-case error is at least \(d_{W+1}(F,C)-\eta\asymp W^{-s/d}-\eta\). The Kolmogorov-width estimate is R; YZ20 §1 states the linear-width version O.

Lemma D (fixed-step training is a continuous selection). P Take a \(C^1\) activation and run \(T\) steps \(\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\nabla_\theta\mathcal L(\theta_t;f)\) of square-loss gradient descent, with fixed initialization, step sizes and seed. Then \(\theta_T(f)\) is continuous in \(f\in(F,\|\cdot\|_\infty)\), and DHM gives worst-case error at least \(c\,W^{-s/d}\) for any \(T\), depth or precision.

  • This does not cover ReLU (the gradient jumps in \(\theta\)), adaptive stopping, or the limit \(T\to\infty\).
  • Borsuk–Ulam gives one bad target per seed. Whether randomized continuous selection beats \(W^{-s/d}\) with high probability for each fixed \(f\) is open to me J.

Verdict. Some assumptions bound \(K\), its local or \(L^2\) versions, or norm products by \(\mathrm{poly}(W)\). Those exclude \(\gamma>1\), and so do fixed-step smooth training (via DHM) and the lazy regime. VC bounds, input-Lipschitzness of the output, and smoothness of a single training objective do not. In the standard theory, the \(W^{-s/d}\) per-parameter barrier comes from these assumptions or from fixed precision, not from the architecture.

§4Predictions for small-network experiments

Testbed. Take \(d=s=1\) and targets

\[ f_\sigma(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma_j\,h\,\varphi\!\Big(\frac{x-x_j}{h}\Big),\qquad \varphi(t)=\big(\tfrac12-|t|\big)_+,\quad h=1/M,\quad \sigma\in\{\pm1\}^M . \]

These form a \(2^M\)-element packing of \(F\) at separation \(h\). Success means sup error below \(h/2\), measured on a grid of at least \(20M\) points.

  1. Counting binds, and trained networks use about \(\log W\) bits per weight.
    • P Any training at any depth succeeds with probability at most \(2^{Wb-M}\), because each network lies within \(h/2\) of at most one target.
    • J For trained networks, the quantization knee \(b^*\) will be about \(c\log_2W\) regardless of depth, so their threshold is \(W^*\asymp M/\log M\).
    • Constructed OSB-style networks should succeed at \(W\asymp\sqrt M\) with depth \(\asymp\sqrt M\). They need exact or multiprecision arithmetic: past \(M\approx3000\) the encoding weights exceed float64's 53-bit mantissa.
    • A trained network whose \(b^*\) grows like a power of \(W\) would falsify this.
  2. \(b_{\rm eff}=\log_2(RK/\varepsilon)\) is the right currency, and trained networks leave the depth's \(K\) budget unused.
    • P \(b^*\le\log_2(RK_{\rm loc}/\varepsilon)+1\) and \(\log K\le\log L+L\log_2((n+1)R)\).
    • J In constructed networks, \(\log K_{\rm loc}\), measured by sup-norm finite differences under weight perturbations, grows linearly in \(L\). In trained networks of the same depths it stays \(O(\log W)\), and the log–log slope of error against \(W\) stays at \(-s/d\) or flatter.
  3. The optimizer caps \(K\). J, motivated by Lemma S.
    • At constructed \(\gamma>1\) solutions, the top Hessian eigenvalue grows like \(e^{cL}\). Gradient descent started there with step \(\eta\gg e^{-cL}\) should leave at once, because edge-of-stability dynamics keep \(\lambda_{\max}\lesssim2/\eta\).
    • At fixed \(W\), sweeping \(\eta\) over six decades should improve the converged error at most polylogarithmically in \(1/\eta\).
    • If the error instead improves like a power of \(1/\eta\), uniform smoothness is not what limits trained networks.

§5Empirical check: adapters on Qwen3-0.6B

This section is measured, not proved, and it reports a pilot scale: one seed, 3,000 Adam steps per run, CPU only.

Task. \(M\) random keys ("482910 ->"), each labelled A or B at random, so the targets are \(M\) fair bits. This is the language-model version of the packing in §4.

Exact computation. The frozen model runs once per item. Training recomputes only the adapted part, which matches the full model's margin to \(2\times10^{-6}\).

Measured per run:

  • stored information \(I=M(1-H_2(\text{err}))\), a Fano estimate;
  • \(I_b\), the same after rounding every adapter weight to \(b\) bits;
  • the knee \(b^*\), the fewest bits per weight that keep error within one point;
  • the rank of the items × parameters gradient matrix.

  • The counting bound held on all 36 runs at every \(b\), as it must: \(I_b\le Wb\). The probe reaches Cover's 2 bits per parameter.
  • Trained LoRA sits far below \(\gamma=1\). An eightfold increase in \(W\) gave about twice the stored bits, a log–log slope of 0.26 against about 1 for the probe.
    • LoRA's stored bits stay within a factor of 1.3 of twice the rank of its gradient matrix at relative threshold \(10^{-3}\).
    • So the binding constraint is the spread of the frozen model's features at the adapter site (Lemma N, in tangent form). Neither \(W\) nor \(b\) is.
  • Precision is spent on conditioning, not information. LoRA needs 7 to 16 bits per weight to keep at most 0.06 bits per parameter, so \(b_{\rm eff}\) runs far above \(I/W\).
  • The knee is predicted by first-order sensitivity.
    • Treating rounding errors as independent noise on the margins predicts the probe's knee within 2 bits on 12 of 12 runs, and exactly on 7.
    • The worst-case prediction from the \(\ell^\infty\) Lipschitz constant (§0.4(ii)) asks for 3 to 4 more bits on the probe's full-fit runs. That is \(\tfrac12\log_2W\), the gap expected between \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^2\) gradient norms.
  • Depth helps capacity, but not through precision. At fixed \(W=16{,}384\) (last-token gate LoRA):
    • Rank 4 in layer 27 alone stored 220 bits.
    • Rank 1 in each of layers 24 to 27 stored all 2,048 labels, with no sign of saturation. That is at least 9 times more per parameter.
    • The four-layer solution needed fewer bits per weight (7 against 8) and was about 100 times flatter (top Hessian eigenvalue 10 against 1,297).
    • What grew was the gradient matrix's rank at \(10^{-2}\), from 16 to 503. So depth bought a richer tangent space, not bit extraction.
    • This contradicts the plain form of conjecture §4.2. It supports its narrower form: trained adapters leave depth's precision (\(K\)) budget unused.
    • Follow-up: placement matters most, spreading adds more.
      • Rank 4 in layer 24 alone also stored all 2,048 labels. Most of the gain over layer 27 comes from where the adapter sits.
      • At \(M=8{,}192\), with both runs stopped at the same 4,000-step cap, the four-layer adapter stored 3,284 bits (0.20 per parameter). That is 3.9 times the 848 bits of the same parameters in layer 24 alone.
      • Both solutions stay flat and need 7 to 8 bits per weight.
